    The problem

    On quick-commerce apps the top few tiles of a search sell most of the volume. A brand's position changes through the day as competitors raise bids, budgets run out and stock moves between dark stores.

    The brand's team could only see this by opening the app on a phone, for one address at a time. On festival days, when a lost hour at the top costs real sales, nobody was watching at 7 AM or on a Sunday.

    What we built
    • Scheduled checks. The brand's chosen searches at chosen delivery addresses, every 10 minutes from 9 AM to 10 PM.
    • Rank and ad slots. For every check: where each of the brand's products sits in the top 30, and which brands hold the sponsored tiles.
    • Live dashboard. Today's positions and history in one page, so the team stops checking the app by hand.
    • Telegram alerts. When a product falls below its target position, the team gets the search, the address, the current position and who took the spot.
    • Gap-filling. If a scheduled check fails, a backup source fills it, so the record has no holes.

    What it means: positions 1 and 5 are a budget fight. The lower sponsored tiles change hands through the day, so that is where timing your bids pays off.

    What we learned
    • A dashboard alone is not enough. A page that shows what the app already shows saves no time. The alerts, and knowing who took the spot, are what the team uses.
    • Alerts follow the brand's hours. The brand runs campaigns in the morning and the evening. Alerts during paused hours are noise and teach people to ignore them.
    • Festival days are the real problem. On ordinary days few bids change. On festival days a slow reaction costs sales, so that is where checks need to be most frequent.
