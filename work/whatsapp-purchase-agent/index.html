The problem

The purchase team buys used corporate laptops from hundreds of traders. Offers arrive on WhatsApp at all hours and in every form: typed text in English, Hindi and Hinglish, voice notes, photos, and Excel or PDF lot lists. A typical typed one: "40 pc 5490 i5 8th 8/256, 14500". A buyer has to read each one, chase the missing details, look up the buying price, reply, haggle and log the result.

On a busy day that means slow replies, offers that never get logged, and good lots that go to a competitor who answered first.

What we built
  • Takes any format. Text, voice notes, photos, Excel sheets and PDFs. Most WhatsApp bots stop at typed text; this one reads whatever a trader sends.
  • Handles big lots. Large lot lists sent as Excel sheets or PDFs are processed in the background, line by line.
  • Reads the offer. Pulls model, processor, generation, RAM, storage, quantity and price out of a loose message in English, Hindi or Hinglish.
  • Asks once. If battery health, grade, chargers or faults are missing, it asks for all of them in one message, not one question at a time.
  • Prices it. Works out a target price from the company's own buying sheet, by model, generation and grade.
  • Negotiates inside limits. Opens a little below target, moves to target, and never goes above the buyer's ceiling.
  • Hands over. Unusual offers (very large lots, unknown models) go straight to a buyer. Every purchase needs a buyer's approval before the agent confirms it.
  • Logs everything. Each offer lands in the team's sheet as a row with its outcome: won, lost or escalated.

How it went
  1. Week 1Read three months of the purchase team's WhatsApp chats to learn how traders write offers and how buyers price them. Agreed the rules: opening counter, ceiling, what counts as unusual.
  2. Week 2Offer reading, pricing against the buying sheet, and the control sheet with its tabs and switches.
  3. Week 3Negotiation and hand-over to buyers. Live demo on WhatsApp with the client's team.
  4. NowThe client is testing it on their own server with their own AI account. Next in build: checking a trader's specs on the web.
What we learned
  • One question beats five. Traders ignore a bot that asks one thing at a time. Asking for every missing detail in a single message gets answers.
  • Humans approve, the agent does the legwork. Buyers trusted it faster once every purchase still needed their tap.
  • The WhatsApp provider was the slowest part. The agent replies in under 10 seconds. Message delivery through the provider sometimes added minutes, so we measure each step separately.
  • A sheet is a fine control panel. The team already lives in spreadsheets. Switches there got used from day one.
